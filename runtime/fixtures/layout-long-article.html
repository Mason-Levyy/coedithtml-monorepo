Sourdough starts with a starter: a jar of flour and water left to ferment until wild yeast takes hold.

Feeding it daily for a week is enough to get a reliable rise, though some bakers keep starters for decades.

The dough itself is mostly patience — folding every thirty minutes for a few hours does more than kneading.

A long cold rise in the fridge overnight develops flavor that a same-day loaf never quite reaches.

Scoring the top right before baking controls where the loaf splits open in the oven.

A cast iron pot with a lid traps steam for the first twenty minutes, giving a crackling crust.
