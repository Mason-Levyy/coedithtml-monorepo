01 — The problem

Artifacts are becoming deliverables. They are still dead ends.

Teams have started sending AI-generated models, dashboards, and decks to colleagues and clients. The moment someone wants to react to one, the workflow collapses.

Inside your orgReal multiplayer editing — if they hold a seat on your plan.
vs.
Everyone elseA view-only link. No editing, no comments, no way to send anything structured back.
And at every tierThere is no comment layer at all. To say "this number looks wrong," you have to change the number.
02 — What it is

A hosted sandbox that adds what the file cannot carry.

Upload a single-file HTML artifact. Get a link. The file is stored byte-for-byte and never modified — everything else is layered on at runtime.

01Read it as a deck

Automatic segmentation turns arbitrary HTML into slides. Filmstrip, arrow keys, works on a phone.

02Comment on it

Sticky notes and text-anchored threads, stored separately from the artifact. Reacting never means editing.

03Edit it directly

Click in and fix the typo, like a doc. No prompting, no regenerating, no waiting on the person with the account.

03 — Who it's for

The person on the other end of the link.

The builder already has tools. The reviewer has nothing — and the reviewer is usually the one whose opinion decides whether the work was any good.

  • Consultants and analysts sending a model or deck to a client who will never have a seat on their plan.
  • Founders putting a prototype in front of advisors and design partners.
  • Anyone on a cross-company project — the sponsor, the counterpart, the contractor — currently locked out by org boundaries.

Zero of these people will install anything, create an account, or learn a tool. The product has to work on the first click or it does not work.

04 — Why it solves the problem

It closes the loop instead of widening it.

Today feedback leaves the artifact as prose in an email and gets re-typed by hand into a prompt. Every regeneration orphans every note.

Share link→ Comment & edit→ Export as prompt→ Regenerate→ Re-anchor

Comments live in a separate overlay keyed to anchors, not to line numbers. When the artifact is rebuilt from scratch, the overlay re-attaches itself and reports honestly on what it could not place. That is the part nobody has built, and it is the part that makes the cycle survivable.

ContrastThe existing collaboration primitive is fork — you get your own copy and the original is untouched. Fork diverges. This converges.
05 — Why it scales

No per-artifact work, and no bet on one model.

01Model-agnostic

The input is a single HTML file. Every tool emits one. No integration, no API keys, no vendor to be deprecated by.

02Zero setup per file

Segmentation is automatic. Nobody tags slides or writes a config. Upload is the entire onboarding.

03Edge economics

Static bytes plus a small coordination object. Marginal cost per artifact is near zero and stays there.

04Rising tide

Volume of AI-generated artifacts only goes one direction. Every one of them eventually needs a second reader.

One primitive does three jobs: the overlay is what renders on screen, what survives a re-upload, and what gets pasted into a model. Features get cheaper as they compound rather than more expensive.

06 — The honest version

This is not a moat. It is a gap, and the gap is open now.

If the model providers decide this matters, they will build it. The bet is not that they can't — it's that cross-tool, no-seat, feedback-first is not the shape any of them are currently building toward.

  • Their sharing is organized around audiences — you, your org, the internet. A client deliverable is none of those.
  • Their collaboration is organized around editing. The missing verb is commenting.
  • Their surface is one model. This one is agnostic by construction.
Worst caseA well-built open-source tool that a lot of people use for a year, and a good answer to "what have you built lately."
